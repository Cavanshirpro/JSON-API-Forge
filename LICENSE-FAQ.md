No, not in the OSI sense. The source is publicly visible and available for inspection, auditing, private modification, and self-hosting, but the license restricts redistribution and alternative distributions. The correct description is **source-available**.

## Can I use it on my own server?

Yes. You may install and run it on infrastructure you control, including a local machine, VPS, cPanel account, cloud instance, private network, or other hosting environment, subject to the license.

## Can I modify it for my own deployment?

Yes. Private/internal modifications are allowed. You may run the privately modified version as your backend or network service as long as you do not give third parties a copy of JSON API Forge or the modified framework.

## Can I use it commercially?

Yes. The license permits commercial use of a privately hosted deployment. Commercial use does **not** grant a right to redistribute the framework itself.

## Can I upload my modified version to GitHub, GitLab, a package registry, Docker Hub, or a download site?

No, unless the Project Owner gives you separate written permission. Public contribution forks are allowed only under the narrow pull-request exception in `LICENSE`.

## Can I send a copy to another person or customer?

No. Link them to the canonical repository or official release page instead. A hosting provider acting only on your behalf may hold a private copy to operate your infrastructure as permitted by the license.

## Can I fork the public GitHub repository?

GitHub permits public repositories to be forked under its platform rules. The project license grants a narrow contribution-fork exception for preparing pull requests. That exception does not authorize you to publish releases, packages, binaries, container images, mirrors, or a separately maintained edition from the fork.

Not without separate written permission from the Project Owner, regardless of whether your edition would be free, paid, source-available, open-source, or proprietary.

You may build and operate a hosted product on top of JSON API Forge, but you may not distribute a product containing a material copy of the framework unless you have separate written authorization.

## Can I inspect the source for security and transparency?

Yes. That is one of the main reasons the project is source-available. Security research, auditing, study, and testing are expressly permitted within the license terms and applicable law.

## Can the project owner change the license later?

Future releases may use different terms. A new license does not silently rewrite the terms attached to a copy you already received. Contributor terms are structured to preserve the Project Owner's ability to relicense future project releases.

## What happens to my existing license if ownership changes?

A lawful ownership transfer does not retroactively remove a license already validly granted to your copy. The successor may administer the transferred rights and choose terms for future releases to the extent it owns or controls the necessary rights.

## How can I obtain redistribution, OEM, or alternative-version rights?

Only through separate written permission or a separate license from the Project Owner.

## Why is there a CLA?

Contributors keep copyright in their contributions, but the CLA gives the Project Owner broad, sublicensable, transferable, assignable, and relicensable rights. That preserves centralized release policy and makes a later transfer of the project to a company more practical.
